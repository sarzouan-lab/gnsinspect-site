Common-expense study: know what your building really costs, and who should pay for what

Condo fees that climb without explanation, repeated special assessments, co-owners disputing the bill: a common-expense study lays the numbers out. Our engineers analyse your operating, maintenance and energy spending and tell you where the money goes and where it is lost.

Request a quote514-965-2842

When to ask for one

  • Before adopting a budget with a significant increase in fees.
  • After the reserve fund study, to align the operating budget with fund contributions.
  • When some expenses benefit only part of the co-owners (parking, elevator, roof terrace, common portions for restricted use).
  • At a change of manager or at the end of the developer’s administration.
Energy is often the first line to fix. Heating of common areas, garage ventilation, central hot water: we cost the possible savings and check the financial assistance available to achieve them.

What the study includes

  1. Expense analysis. Three to five years of financial statements and invoices, line by line.
  2. Comparison. Your costs per unit and per square metre against similar buildings.
  3. Maintenance contracts. Elevators, mechanical, snow removal, cleaning: what is paid for, what is actually done.
  4. Energy and water use. Consumption profile of the common portions and costed savings.
  5. Expense allocation. Identifying expenses tied to common portions for restricted use, to be allocated per the declaration of co-ownership.
  6. Recommended budget. A realistic operating budget, aligned with the maintenance log and the reserve fund.

Frequently asked questions

Can the study change how fees are split among co-owners?

No. The split follows the relative value of the fractions set in the declaration of co-ownership; changing it takes a notary and a vote of the assembly. The study shows what should be charged to whom under the existing declaration.

Is it mandatory?

No. It is a management tool, unlike the maintenance log and the reserve fund study, which the law requires.

How does it differ from the reserve fund study?

The reserve fund pays for major repairs and replacements. Common expenses pay for day-to-day operation. The two studies complement each other.

How long does it take?

Three to six weeks, depending on the quality of the accounting records.
